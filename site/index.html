How it works

What the model looks at

Every forecast comes from a logistic regression that gives each match three chances: home win, draw and away win. It uses only what is known before kick-off:

  • Elo ratings: a running strength score for every team, updated after each result.
  • Expected goals (xG): how many chances each team has been creating and allowing over its last 20 league games, from Opta data published by Fantasy Premier League.
  • Bookmaker odds: turned into probabilities, when they have been published. Until then the forecast uses ratings and xG alone.

The model is retrained every week on every match played since 2017, and this page rebuilds itself every Tuesday and Friday morning.

How it was tested

To get an honest score, the model was replayed week by week over 1,240 matches from August 2023 to November 2026. Before each week it was retrained on earlier matches only, then asked to predict that week.

Why it never predicts a draw

Roughly one in four Premier League matches ends level: 24.7% of the 1,240 matches in the test. Yet in none of them was the draw the single most likely outcome. Not for this model, and not for the bookmakers, whose draw probability never rose above 32%.

This isn't a blind spot. A draw is most likely when two teams are evenly matched, but evenly matched also means each win stays close to a third, so the draw is almost never the favourite. A model that maximises accuracy always picks the most likely result, so it never picks a draw, and it accepts that about a quarter of matches will be wrong whatever it does.

That's why accuracy alone is a weak test of a forecaster. The draw chance still appears on every match, and the log loss scores whether those percentages are right, not just whether the top pick came in.

What didn't help

  • Random forests, gradient boosting, nearest neighbours and a small neural network all scored worse than logistic regression.
  • Days of rest and fixture congestion, including Champions League, Europa League and cup games, changed nothing beyond noise.
  • Extra signals for draws (goal expectations, how evenly matched the teams are) did not improve the draw percentages.

Where the data comes from

  • Results, match stats and odds: football-data.co.uk
  • Newest results and fixtures: ESPN
  • Expected goals and gameweeks: Fantasy Premier League
